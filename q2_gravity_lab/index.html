After a burn, E, L and P change by exactly the work, angular impulse and impulse of the external force; the small residuals are the integrator's own (bounded) error.

Both runs start from the current initial conditions with the same fixed step and the same simulated duration. Velocity-Verlet is symplectic: its energy error oscillates (largest near periapsis) but does not grow. RK4 is 4th-order, so over short runs at the same Δt it is usually more accurate, but it is not symplectic and its energy error grows steadily with time. Example (preset D, Δt × 64): after 20 periods RK4's error (≈7×10⁻²) is below Verlet's (≈2×10⁻¹); after 500 periods Verlet's is unchanged while RK4's has grown by orders of magnitude.

Geosynchronous vs geostationary. A geosynchronous orbit has a period equal to one sidereal day (86 164.1 s), so the satellite returns to the same point in the sky once per day; if it is inclined or eccentric it traces a figure-8 or tear-drop ground track. A geostationary orbit is the special geosynchronous orbit that is circular (e = 0) and equatorial (i = 0): the satellite then stays fixed above one ground longitude. This planar simulator shows the equatorial case. Raise the orbit (Δr > 0) and the satellite drifts westward (longer period); lower it and it drifts eastward.

Physics Behind the Simulation

1 · Newton's law of universal gravitation

F12 = G m₁ m₂ (r₂ − r₁) / r³ , |F| = G m₁ m₂ / r²

Each mass attracts the other along the line of centres. The force on body 1 points toward body 2 and the force on body 2 is equal and opposite. Accelerations: a₁ = +Gm₂(r₂−r₁)/r³, a₂ = −Gm₁(r₂−r₁)/r³.

2 · Centre of mass

R = (m₁r₁ + m₂r₂)/(m₁+m₂), M V = P

With no external force, M d²R/dt² = 0, so the COM moves at constant velocity. The simulator starts with P = 0 and R = 0, so the COM stays fixed at the origin. Body positions about the COM: r₁ = −(m₂/M)r, r₂ = +(m₁/M)r.

3 · Reduced mass & equivalent one-body problem

μ = m₁m₂/(m₁+m₂), μ d²r/dt² = −G m₁m₂ r/r³

Subtracting the two equations of motion gives a single body of mass μ moving about a fixed centre with gravitational parameter μ_g = G(m₁+m₂). Both real bodies trace scaled copies of this relative orbit about the COM (scale factors m₂/M and m₁/M).

4 · Conservation of mechanical energy

E = ½m₁v₁² + ½m₂v₂² − G m₁m₂/r = constant

Gravity is conservative (F = −∇U), so kinetic and potential energy exchange while their sum stays constant. In the COM frame E = μ ε with specific orbital energy ε = v²/2 − μ_g/r.

5 · Conservation of angular momentum

L = Σ mᵢ rᵢ × vᵢ , dL/dt = Σ rᵢ × Fᵢ = 0

The forces are central and act along the same line, so their total torque about the COM is zero. The specific relative angular momentum h = |r × v| = r² dθ/dt is constant — this is Kepler's second law.

6 · Vis-viva equation

v² = μ_g ( 2/r − 1/a )

Follows from energy conservation with ε = −μ_g/(2a). It gives the relative speed at any separation r. At periapsis v_p = √(μ_g(1+e)/r_p); at apoapsis v_a = √(μ_g(1−e)/r_a), so v_p/v_a = r_a/r_p = (1+e)/(1−e).

7 · Orbital eccentricity

e = [ (v² − μ_g/r) r − (r·v) v ] / μ_g , e = √(1 + 2εh²/μ_g²)

The eccentricity vector points to periapsis. e = 0 circle, 0 < e < 1 ellipse (ε < 0), e = 1 parabola (ε = 0), e > 1 hyperbola (ε > 0). r_p = h²/[μ_g(1+e)], r_a = h²/[μ_g(1−e)].

8 · Circular orbital velocity

v_c = √( μ_g / r )

Gravity supplies exactly the centripetal force: μ v²/r = G m₁m₂/r². Any other tangential speed at the same r produces an ellipse with that point as periapsis (v > v_c) or apoapsis (v < v_c).

9 · Escape velocity

v_esc = √( 2 μ_g / r ) = √2 · v_c

The speed at which ε = 0: kinetic energy just equals the depth of the potential well. v < v_esc → bound (ellipse); v = v_esc → parabola; v > v_esc → hyperbola with excess speed v_∞ = √(v² − v_esc²). The direction of launch does not matter for escape, only the speed.

10 · Kepler's three laws

r = a(1−e²)/(1+e cos θ) ; dA/dt = h/2 ; T² = 4π²a³/[G(m₁+m₂)]

(1) Bound orbits are ellipses with the central mass (in the two-body problem, the COM) at a focus. (2) The radius vector sweeps equal areas in equal times, because h is constant. (3) The square of the period is proportional to the cube of the semi-major axis; note the exact form uses m₁+m₂, not just the central mass.

11 · Geosynchronous orbital radius

G M m / r² = m ω² r , ω = 2π/T ⇒ r_geo = [ G M T² / 4π² ]1/3

Setting the orbital period equal to Earth's sidereal rotation period (86 164.1 s, rotation relative to the stars — not the 86 400 s solar day) gives r_geo ≈ 42 164 km, i.e. an altitude of about 35 786 km, with circular speed ≈ 3.075 km/s.

Numerical integration

Verlet: vn+½ = vn + ½anΔt ; xn+1 = xn + vn+½Δt ; vn+1 = vn+½ + ½an+1Δt

Velocity-Verlet is 2nd-order, time-reversible and symplectic; it conserves linear and angular momentum to round-off for central forces and keeps energy error bounded. RK4 is 4th-order but not symplectic. As a safety net near close approaches a step is subdivided only when Δt > 0.3·√(r³/μ_g). Variable steps destroy the symplectic property, so the net is set to trigger only when a step would otherwise be dangerously coarse; the status bar reports when it acts.
